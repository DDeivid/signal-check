## White House Energy Truce Collapses

**Mia:** The diplomatic rift between Washington and Kyiv widened sharply this week after Donald Trump condemned Ukraine for launching fresh drone strikes against Russian oil refineries. In an interview broadcast on Newsmax on Thursday, the US president called the attacks a disgrace and argued they directly push up global diesel prices.

**Theo:** Trump went quite a bit further than simply talking about fuel markets. He stated explicitly that he is not a big fan of Zelenskyy, and claimed he finds the Ukrainian president more difficult to deal with than Vladimir Putin. That is an extraordinary statement to make publicly about an ally defending its territory in an invasion that has now lasted more than four and a half years.

**Mia:** The backdrop here is critical. Last week, Trump announced what he termed an energy ceasefire between Russia and Ukraine. But according to reporting, neither Moscow nor Kyiv had actually signed on to that deal. Around the same time, Trump eased US sanctions on Russia in exchange for Moscow releasing large quantities of diesel into global markets, a step widely seen as aimed at taming fuel prices ahead of the US midterm elections.

**Theo:** And Zelenskyy rejected that deal outright. His position is that providing sanctions relief gives Moscow a direct financial lifeline to press on with the war. Kyiv announced it was abandoning the truce Trump declared, arguing that hitting Russian refining capacity is essential to choking off Russian military logistics and starving the Kremlin's war economy.

**Mia:** Kyiv also pointed out that Russian strikes on Ukrainian infrastructure never actually stopped. Russian drones hit energy facilities in the city of Lutsk, triggering widespread blackouts. Meanwhile, Moscow used the opportunity to claim that Ukraine was sabotaging American-led peace initiatives by restarting refinery strikes.

**Theo:** Tensions grew even worse earlier in the week when Ukrainian drone operations and air defenses were suddenly crippled after Starlink satellite services were restricted. In London, Zelenskyy said the cutoff made him feel personally betrayed by Trump. British Defence Secretary Wes Streeting met US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, who gave assurances that Starlink had been restored. But Streeting noted it remains completely unclear whether that disruption was an intentional political squeeze by Washington or simply a technical failure.

**Mia:** European allies have spent the last few days attempting to reassure Kyiv amid the fallout. French President Emmanuel Macron met Zelenskyy in Brussels, stressing that it is crucial Ukraine receives all necessary means to defend itself. Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk posted a public message reading simply, you are not alone.

**Theo:** All eyes are on Brussels on Friday, where Zelenskyy is attending an EU summit. European leaders will be looking to see what concrete security and energy backstops they can offer Ukraine, especially as Washington's posture grows increasingly unpredictable.

## Tech Wreck Drives Defensive Rotation

**Mia:** Turning to financial markets, the selloff in artificial intelligence shares deepened on Thursday, triggering what market participants are calling an outright tech wreck. According to MarketWatch, institutional investors are dumping high-flying tech names and parking capital in government bonds and defensive stock sectors.

**Theo:** You can see that strain clearly in the tape. The Nasdaq 100 closed Thursday at 29935.60 USD, down 0.05% on the day, but that extends its five-session slide to a negative 2.57%. Meanwhile, the broader S&P 500 managed a 0.33% gain on the day to close at 7744.06 USD, though it remains down 0.27% over five sessions, largely because money rotated directly into value and defensive groups.

**Mia:** This rotation is really the culmination of questions that have been building for several quarters. Ever since generative AI captured mainstream attention back in 2022 and 2023, mega-cap technology companies have poured staggering sums into capital expenditures. They've bought advanced semiconductors, constructed massive data centers, and secured heavy electrical power capacity.

**Theo:** And the skeptical argument on Wall Street is finally hitting home: where is the cash flow? Capital spending on hardware and concrete has outpaced near-term enterprise software monetization. When tech companies trade at historically stretched valuation multiples, any delay in converting infrastructure into high-margin recurring revenue leaves them vulnerable to steep corrections. Why take that risk when sovereign bonds offer solid, guaranteed yields?

**Mia:** Long-term AI advocates argue this pullback is just normal market volatility rather than a broken business model. Their view is that every foundational platform shift, whether the personal computer, the internet, or mobile computing, demands years of upfront infrastructure spending before the software ecosystem can mature and monetize.

**Theo:** That may be true historically, but the reporting notes we still don't know whether this is a brief portfolio rebalancing or the start of a prolonged structural de-rating. The key tests will come with upcoming corporate earnings reports. We need to watch management guidance on capex budgets and see if enterprise customers are actually paying for generative AI software at scale.

## Prediction check and weekend picks

**Theo:** No predictions were made this week, so we can jump straight into our weekend reading picks.

**Mia:** My first pick is an article in Quanta Magazine featuring evolutionary biologist Toby Kiers, looking at the vital role fungal networks play beneath the soil. Kiers explains how these underground fungal networks trade nutrients with plant roots and help keep carbon underground.

**Theo:** What makes that piece compelling is how these networks strike bargains and trade resources, all without having a brain. It fundamentally shifts how you think about biological economics, underground nutrient flows, and soil health.

**Mia:** My second pick heads into open-source hardware hacking. CNX Software covered a new project called w1700k-sdr from developer h0m3us3r. The project converts an off-the-shelf Gemtek W1700K Wi-Fi 7 router into a wideband software-defined radio receiver.

**Theo:** This is clever engineering. Previously, radio enthusiasts experimented with turning low-cost microcontrollers like the ESP32 into software-defined radios, but serial data connections were far too slow to handle high sample rates. This new project taps into the router's internal MediaTek MT7996 radio chip to capture raw fourteen-bit samples at up to 320 mega-samples per second across the 2.4, 5, and 6 gigahertz bands, streaming that massive data stream out over a ten-gigabit Ethernet link.

**Mia:** It essentially turns consumer networking hardware into an extremely capable laboratory-grade radio scanner for anyone wanting to inspect wide slices of the wireless spectrum.

**Theo:** Finally, my pick for the weekend is an essay in Aeon by Alexander V Gheorghiu titled The Ladder of Knowledge. It traces the century-long philosophical and scientific pursuit to establish the absolute limits of human understanding.

**Mia:** Gheorghiu's central argument is really refreshing. He proposes that truth shouldn't be thought of as a fixed, unreachable ceiling that humanity constantly fails to attain. Instead, what we define as true is an evolving framework that adapts and expands as we climb higher up the ladder of discovery.

**Theo:** It is a thoughtful look at epistemic humility, which is especially useful when sorting through noisy headlines and competing technical claims.
