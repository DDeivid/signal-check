## Starlink Battlefield Limits

**Mia:** We start today with growing friction between private tech monopolies and modern warfare. A bipartisan group of US senators has signed a letter demanding an investigation into unexpected speed restrictions on SpaceX's Starlink network in Ukraine.

**Theo:** The letter was sent on Thursday to the US Department of Defense inspector general, and it was spearheaded by Democratic Senator Jeanne Shaheen. The lawmakers say they have grave concern over reported speed limits imposed between October 12 and 14.

**Mia:** According to the BBC, Starlink restricted speeds on Tuesday night to one hundred miles per hour, meaning any weapons travelling faster than that lost connectivity. Ukraine says that disrupted its ability to defend against Russian drone and missile attacks on civilian infrastructure.

**Theo:** The senators also cited disruptions affecting intelligence-sharing and threat-warning capabilities, noting incidents where US personnel couldn't reach bunkers during air raids. They want the inspector general to find out whether the Pentagon or its contractors caused the slowdown.

**Mia:** Elon Musk suggested on X that the US government may be behind the restrictions, pointing out that government-controlled Starshield systems dictate limits set by the US military and the president.

**Theo:** And that ties directly into broader geopolitical friction. President Volodymyr Zelensky accused the US of pulling back intelligence-sharing to pressure Kyiv into ending attacks against Russian energy sites, following an energy ceasefire proposed by Donald Trump that the Kremlin never officially agreed to.

**Mia:** So far, the White House and Pentagon haven't responded to suggestions that the US played a role in the Starlink slowdown. It's a stark reminder of how much leverage a private provider holds when its hardware underpins a sovereign nation's defense.

**Theo:** We'll be watching to see if the Department of Defense inspector general officially launches an investigation into the Starlink disruptions.

## Linus Torvalds' First AI Patch

**Mia:** Next up: a cultural turning point in open-source systems programming. Linus Torvalds has officially posted his first LLM-assisted patch to a Linux kernel development branch.

**Theo:** Tom's Hardware reports that the patch targets an old problem: severe CPU cache contention when multiple processor cores simultaneously read the same file, which usually hurts databases, shared libraries, and web servers.

**Mia:** Torvalds wrote an initial draft solution back in early 2024 and ran it on his personal laptop, but recently decided to use an LLM to try to solve it properly. In a dedicated synthetic benchmark, he claims it clocked a twenty-three times performance improvement.

**Theo:** Notice the heavy caveat he added, though. He noted that real-world impact will be far less significant because no real load will replicate those synthetic conditions. If it helps anyone, it will be server workloads rather than desktop machines.

**Mia:** This follows a clear pro-AI shift from Torvalds. Back in July, he said he was willing to put his foot down against AI critics, arguing that automated code review tools used by the kernel team already outperform humans by catching basic programming mistakes.

**Theo:** And the project is actually changing its policy to drop the requirement that patch authors must disclose if they used an AI tool. Torvalds treats large language models as ordinary productivity tools, comparable to compilers or text editors, as long as the programmer understands what they are doing.

**Mia:** Not everyone is completely comfortable with this shift, however. Earlier this year, automated bug-hunting agents scouring the Linux kernel made the security mailing list unmanageable, and maintainers have even been forced to drop support for older hardware drivers just to keep up with security alerts.

**Theo:** We still don't know which specific LLM Torvalds used, or when or if this development branch patch will ever be merged into the official mainline kernel.
