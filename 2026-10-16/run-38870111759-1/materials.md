1. What Happened Precisely

On October 15, 2026, Tom's Hardware reported that Dutch semiconductor equipment manufacturer ASML posted record financial results for the third quarter of 2026. 

Key figures reported by ASML:
- Revenue: $13.06 billion (11.66 billion euros), an increase of 55 percent year-over-year and 25 percent sequentially.
- Gross Margin: A record 57.5 percent, up from 51.6 percent in Q3 2025.
- Operating Income: $5.79 billion (5.17 billion euros).
- Net Income: $5.56 billion (4.96 billion euros). This figure includes approximately $570 million (510 million euros) from a re-evaluation of ASML's investment in Mistral AI.
- Installed Base Management (service and upgrades): Generated $3.68 billion, roughly $450 million above guidance, fueled by demand for machine productivity upgrades.

Shipments and customer breakdown:
- ASML sold 121 lithography systems during Q3 (113 new, 8 refurbished), up from 91 systems in Q2 2026.
- EUV tools: 17 systems sold, generating $4.59 billion. 
- DUV and other tools: 85 deep ultraviolet (DUV) systems (including 45 ArFi scanners) and 19 i-line systems, generating $4.82 billion. (Revenue from one High-NA EUV system was recognized earlier, in Q2).
- End markets: Logic chipmakers accounted for 54 percent of equipment revenue; memory chipmakers accounted for 46 percent.
- Geography: Taiwan represented 32 percent of equipment sales, South Korea 31 percent, China 23 percent, the United States 13 percent, and the rest of Asia 1 percent.

Guidance:
- Fourth quarter 2026 revenue is projected between $15.9 billion and $17.0 billion, with gross margin around 57 percent.
- Full-year 2026 revenue is expected to reach $49.3 billion to $50.4 billion (a roughly 36 percent year-over-year increase), with EUV revenue up more than 45 percent and memory-related equipment sales up roughly 75 percent.


2. Background and How We Got Here

ASML holds an effective global monopoly on extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography systems, which are essential for printing the microscopic circuitry used in the world's most advanced processors and memory chips. Because these machines are massive, highly complex, and take months to assemble and ship, tool availability is one of the most critical bottlenecks in the global semiconductor supply chain.

The massive surge in global artificial intelligence infrastructure investments has accelerated demand for both high-end logic chips (such as AI accelerators and advanced processors) and advanced memory (notably DRAM and High Bandwidth Memory). To meet this demand, leading semiconductor manufacturers have been forced to plan their factory builds years in advance, reserving equipment build-slots well ahead of physical construction.


3. Perspectives and Arguments

ASML's Perspective:
Chief Executive Officer Christophe Fouquet stated during the company's investor call that leading-edge customers have unprecedented long-term visibility due to commitments from their own buyers. According to Fouquet, customers expect demand to outpace chip supply through at least 2028. Because of this, ASML reports that clients are already placing orders for delivery in 2028 and expressing interest in booking slots for 2029 and 2030. 

Chipmakers' Perspective and Tensions:
Fabricators in Taiwan and South Korea, which together represent 63 percent of ASML's equipment revenue, are committing enormous capital to ensure they are not cut out of future manufacturing capacity. However, client friction exists beneath the surface: Tom's Hardware references reports that planned Low-NA EUV price increases by ASML have caused frustration among key customers, specifically TSMC. Customers face the risk of committing vast sums years ahead for tools that carry escalating price tags.

It is unclear from the available text what specific further capacity expansions or constraints ASML is forecasting beyond its planned 30 percent increases in 2027 and 2028.
- Firmness of far-out demand: While ASML states customers are asking for 2029 and 2030 delivery slots, it is not established how many of these inquiries are binding contractual purchase orders versus non-binding horizon planning or precautionary over-booking.
- Customer pricing disputes: Details regarding the severity of TSMC's reported pushback against Low-NA EUV price hikes remain unclear in the provided text.


5. What to Watch Next

- Delivery and production ramp: Whether ASML meets its target to produce around 65 Low-NA EUV systems in 2026 (up from 44 in 2025) and whether it formally commits to raising capacity to roughly 85 units in 2027 and 110 in 2028.
- Q4 2026 performance: Tracking whether ASML hits its projected $15.9 billion to $17.0 billion revenue target for the final quarter of 2026.
- Memory market momentum: Monitoring whether memory equipment revenue actually achieves the projected 75 percent annual growth, indicating sustained expansion in AI-driven DRAM capacity.
- Geographic shifts: Watching whether the United States increases its share of equipment deliveries beyond 13 percent as heavily subsidized domestic fabs near tool-installation phases.

Podcast Research Brief: Trump Criticizes Ukraine Over Refineries and Ceasefire Collapse

1. What Happened, Precisely

In an interview aired Thursday, October 15, 2026, on the Newsmax news network, US President Donald Trump called Ukraine’s resumption of drone strikes on Russian oil refineries a "disgrace." 

Trump stated that Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy is driving up global oil prices and remarked, "I think I find Zelenskyy to be more difficult to deal with than Putin."

These comments follow the collapse of a short-lived "energy ceasefire" that Trump abruptly announced the prior week. Ukraine formally abandoned the arrangement, leading both nations to trade strikes once again. Zelenskyy reported that a Russian drone strike recently hit energy infrastructure in the Ukrainian city of Lutsk, causing blackouts. Meanwhile, a file photo from September 20, 2026, showed smoke from a previous Ukrainian drone strike on a refinery in Moscow.


2. Background and Context

Russia launched its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022, over four and a half years prior to these events. US efforts under President Trump to end the conflict have produced no tangible progress.

In early October 2026, Trump declared an "energy ceasefire" between Russia and Ukraine. However, neither Kyiv nor Moscow publicly stated that they had formally agreed to the truce. 

As part of his initiative, Trump eased US sanctions on Russia after Moscow agreed to release large amounts of diesel onto global markets. Observers interpreted this move as an attempt to lower fuel prices ahead of the upcoming US midterm elections. Zelenskyy strongly condemned the sanctions relief, asserting that it provides Russia with resources to continue its military campaign. 

Relations worsened further during the week of October 12, 2026, when Ukraine experienced widespread disruptions to its Starlink satellite communications, which severely impacted Ukrainian military drone operations and air defenses.


3. Reactions and Strongest Arguments

US President Donald Trump:
Argued that Ukraine's strikes on Russian diesel refineries harm global interests. He stated: "When you shoot at diesel refineries where you make... diesel oil, and we need that, and the world needs that, then he’s not doing a good service for the world." He added that he is "not a big fan of Zelenskyy."

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy:
Argued that relaxing sanctions on Russia enables Moscow to continue its invasion. During a visit to London, Zelenskyy stated that the sudden restriction of Starlink services made him feel "personally betrayed" by Trump.

The Kremlin:
Accused Ukraine of derailing US peace efforts by choosing to resume strikes on Russian energy infrastructure.

British Defence Secretary Wes Streeting:
Spoke to the BBC on Thursday, October 15, 2026, following a face-to-face meeting with US Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth. Streeting stated Hegseth assured him that Starlink services had been restored to Ukraine, though Streeting noted that "this is not the first time we’ve seen a falling out between Washington and Kyiv."

European Leaders:
Offered firm backing to Kyiv amid growing doubts about long-term US support. 
- NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte met with Zelenskyy in Brussels.
- French President Emmanuel Macron stated after a meeting with Zelenskyy that it is "crucial" for Ukraine to receive "all the means necessary to protect itself."
- Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk publicly messaged Zelenskyy online, writing: "YOU ARE NOT ALONE."


4. What Is Still Unknown or Disputed

- Cause of Starlink Disruption: British Defence Secretary Wes Streeting stated on October 15, 2026, that it remains unclear what caused the sudden restriction of Starlink satellite services in Ukraine.
- Legality/Status of the Ceasefire: Trump referred to the arrangement as an active ceasefire, but neither Ukraine nor Russia acknowledged formally signing or agreeing to its terms before strikes resumed.
- Future of US Diplomatic Strategy: It remains unclear if the US administration plans additional sanctions adjustments or further unilateral diplomatic proposals regarding the war.


5. What to Watch Next

- EU Summit Outcomes: Zelenskyy was scheduled to participate in an EU summit on Friday, October 16, 2026, where European allies may announce concrete measures or formal assurances to back Ukraine.
- Global Oil Prices and US Politics: The economic impact of continued Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries, particularly regarding diesel prices leading into the US midterm elections.
- Frontline Infrastructure Impact: Escalating retaliation against energy grids, including further Ukrainian drone strikes on Russian refining capacity and Russian attacks on Ukrainian electricity networks.
